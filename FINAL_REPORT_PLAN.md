## 3. Remaining engineering — completing the engine for the ablation

The ablation needs a decoder block we can toggle. That requires three things not yet in `src/`: **LayerNorm**, **attention in our Tensor engine**, and the **block wiring**. Each lands with a numerical gradient check *before* it is "done" (the non-negotiable from PLAN.md).

### 3.1 Scaled dot-product attention in the Tensor engine
- Port Luis's `attention_backward.md` derivation into `src/jacobigrad/nn/attention.py` using our `Tensor` ops (single-head, causal mask).
- The op surface already supports it: batched `@`, `log_softmax`/softmax, broadcasting, reductions. The only possibly-missing primitive is a **stable row softmax returning probabilities** (we have `log_softmax`; add `softmax` or reuse) and masking via additive `-inf`.
- **Validation:** grad-check the whole attention forward against finite differences, **and** assert parity against Luis's `closed_form_backward` / `torch_attention` oracle at `float64`, `1e-9`. The oracle already exists — this is the payoff of his contribution.

### 3.2 LayerNorm (forward + backward)
- `src/jacobigrad/nn/layernorm.py`: `y = γ·(x−μ)/√(σ²+ε) + β` over the feature axis.
- Backward: derive the closed form (the three-term gradient through mean and variance) **or** compose it from existing autograd ops and let the engine differentiate it — compare both as a learning check. Document the derivation in `writeups/cs229/` alongside the attention note.
- **Validation:** grad-check + torch `nn.LayerNorm` parity test.

### 3.3 Decoder block with toggles
- `src/jacobigrad/nn/block.py`: single-head self-attention + position-wise MLP, with two switches:
  - **LayerNorm placement** ∈ {pre, post, none}
  - **residual** ∈ {on, off}
- This is the experimental apparatus. Keep it tiny (the thesis is gradient flow, not SOTA perplexity).
- **Validation:** full-block grad-check; one end-to-end torch-parity construction for the {pre-norm, residual-on} config as a sanity anchor.

> Scope holds: **single-head, Tiny Shakespeare only, one seed per config** (per PLAN.md non-negotiables). Multi-head and multi-seed remain explicitly out.

## 4. The experiment — gradient-flow ablation (the thesis)

### 4.1 Grid
3 norm-placements × 2 residual settings = **6 configurations**, trained identically (same data, seed, optimizer, step budget) so the only variable is the architectural toggle.

### 4.2 Instrumentation (this is the novel contribution — the "gradient-flow" in the title)
Logged across training, per config:
- **Per-layer gradient norms** (and gradient-norm ratios across depth) — the central diagnostic.
- **Activation norms** per layer.
- **Jacobian / signal-propagation statistics** (e.g. singular-value spread of the block Jacobian, or grad-norm decay rate with depth) — pick one rigorous measure and justify it.
- Training/val CE vs steps; steps-to-threshold.

Add a lightweight metrics-logging hook to the training loop; dump per-config JSON to `experiments/cs229/results/ablation/`.

### 4.3 Benchmark vs PyTorch (supporting, not central)
Per the scope-cut order, keep at minimum **val-perplexity parity** against an equivalent torch block. If time allows: steps-to-threshold, peak memory, wallclock/step.

### 4.4 Acceptance for the experiment
- All 6 configs train to completion with logged gradient-flow metrics.
- The results **show a legible story** about how norm placement × residual govern gradient propagation (e.g. post-norm-no-residual degrades, pre-norm-residual is stable) — even a clean *negative*/expected result is a valid finding, stated honestly.
- Val-perplexity parity with torch holds for at least the anchor config.
